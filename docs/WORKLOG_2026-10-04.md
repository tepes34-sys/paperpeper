### Step 7 — implemented, reviewed, and merged

The offline implementation includes append-only ledger entries, per-tick reconciliation from three paths, minimal stale/mismatch alerts, and human-only administrative VOID with dry-run and the shared global lock. Migration was tested on synthetic databases and offline copies; no operating database was migrated.

Cash is compared against ledger totals and an independently reconstructed fill calculation. The conservation identity is:

```text
cash + active entry cost = initial cash + realized gross P&L
```

This replaces an invalid inequality that rejected valid states after profitable exits. VOID cancellation is reconstructed from original entry fills, position state, and administrative evidence, without reusing the compensating-ledger amount in the independent fill path.

Existing mismatches remain visible, block new entries, and allow exits only when their evidence can be validated without enlarging the mismatch. A newly introduced mismatch rolls back the entire trading tick, while a separate transaction preserves failure evidence and collection remains committed. No silent cash correction or fabricated HALT transition is applied.

VOID changes the position, compensating entry, cash, administrative event, **all connected unresolved stale alerts**, and each resolution event in one transaction. Failure injection verifies rollback across those changes.

### Independent findings and retry interpretation

Independent review found no remaining blocking issue after repairs. A medium finding compared equivalent Decimal values by text, incorrectly treating a changed number of trailing digits as a new mismatch and rolling back valid exits and the other strategy. Signatures now compare values. Two additional regressions detect compensating entries attached to non-VOID positions and preserve evidence for negative cash rather than failing silently. A minor alert-grouping observation remains for step 8.

Three retry cases are explicitly distinct:

1. A persistent defect within the same LIVE window rejects the same tick again, without duplicate failure evidence.
2. After defect removal, a later RECOVERED retry can commit without recreating the missed entry.
3. With an entry-only defect still present, RECOVERED may also commit because it omits entry creation. The next LIVE entry detects the defect again, and the original evidence remains.

An alert resolved because the tick committed records that reason and **does not claim the underlying defect was fixed**. Recovered completion, defect repair, and later detection are separate observations.
